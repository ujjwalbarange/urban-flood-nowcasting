# Benchmark Historical Flood Event: 26 July 2005 Mumbai Deluge

## 1. Meteorological Overview
- **Date**: 26 July 2005 (08:30 IST on 26 July to 08:30 IST on 27 July)
- **Extreme 24-Hour Rainfall**: 944.2 mm at IMD Santacruz observatory.
- **Maximum 4-Hour Cloudburst**: 400 mm recorded between 14:30 and 18:30 IST.
- **Spatial Gradient**: Colaba recorded only 73.4 mm during the same 24-hour period, demonstrating an unprecedented meso-scale convective micro-burst centered over suburban Mumbai.
- **Synoptic Mechanism**: Stationary offshore vortex coupled with strong mid-tropospheric westerly jet off the Konkan coast.

## 2. Hydrological and Coastal Aggravation
- **Astronomical Spring Tide**: Coincided with a spring high tide of 4.48 meters (above Chart Datum) at 15:50 IST.
- **Flap Gate Closure & Estuarine Backflow**: The 4.48m sea level exceeded outfall inverts across the Mithi River mouth (Mahim Bay), causing full tidal locking and violent saline water intrusion into low-lying inland channels.
- **Mithi River Overbank Flooding**: The river overflowed its banks across a 15 km reach, inundating Chhatrapati Shivaji International Airport runway, Kalina, Kurla, Saki Naka, and Bandra-Kurla Complex under 1.5 to 3.5 meters of water.

## 3. Human and Infrastructural Impact
- Fatalities: 400+ within Mumbai city, over 1,000 across Maharashtra.
- Transport Breakdown: Over 150,000 passengers stranded at railway stations; 10,000 buses and 30,000 private vehicles submerged.
- Communication Blackout: Telecom exchanges inundated due to ground-floor power failures.

## 4. Key Lessons for Hydrodynamic Nowcasting
- 1D-2D coupling is non-negotiable: Water does not remain in drains; overland street propagation across macro-depressions dominates.
- Tidal boundary conditions dictate discharge capacity: Flap gate status must dynamically track sea level.
- Spatial radar ingestion is imperative: Point rain gauges at Colaba failed to signal the suburban disaster.
